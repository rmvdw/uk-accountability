About this site

A free, independent reference that uses official public data to answer questions people are asking about the UK: what the numbers show, how they have changed and how the UK compares with similar countries.

Who runs it

It is built and run by {{ site.owner }}, in a personal capacity. It is independent.

How it is made

AI assistants (Claude, made by Anthropic, and ChatGPT and Codex, made by OpenAI) write the code and draft the pages. Changes to code, definitions and wording are reviewed by a second assistant, and new indicators, published briefings and their wording need {{ site.owner.split()[0] }}'s approval. Assistant review is not independent expert or human fact-checking.

Routine updates to figures for already-approved indicators are made by ordinary code that downloads the official data and checks it automatically before anything changes; no AI is involved in updating numbers.

What we try to do

  • Say plainly what the data show, and explain rather than argue for any policy.
  • Describe what happened, without claiming what caused it. The figures alone do not show how much credit or blame belongs to any government.
  • Where estimates are contested, show the credible range and why it differs. Studies of different people, periods or outcomes are not ends of one range.
  • Compare countries only on the same definition and the same year, and say when that isn't possible.
  • Keep the UK, Great Britain, England, Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland distinct. Great Britain excludes Northern Ireland, and some official figures cover only Great Britain or only England; each page says what it covers.

Uncertainty and revisions

Many official figures are provisional and are revised later. Pages label provisional and estimated figures. Briefings also say what changed since the previous release, and each published version of a briefing (an "edition") keeps its own permanent address and downloadable figures, so a citation still works after later updates. Indicator pages show the latest figures and when their source was last checked, and say so if a check is late.

Data, licences and code

The site's code is open source under the MIT licence. Indicator definitions and the saved source files are available in the repository. Source data and third-party evidence (such as surveys) keep their own licences and reuse terms, which each page credits; the MIT licence does not cover them.
