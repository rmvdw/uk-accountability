Greater Manchester: buses and transport

What the mayor is responsible for, with what money, and what has happened, compared with the other {{ n - 1 }} areas in England with an elected regional mayor. Bus figures: Department for Transport, year to March {{ latest }} (revised March 2026). Next figures due {{ src.next_release }}.

What does the mayor run?

The buses, taken over from 2023 to 2025. The Mayor and the Combined Authority set transport strategy and the transport budget. Transport for Greater Manchester manages the Bee Network of buses and Metrolink trams.

Buses moved into public control in three stages, from September 2023 to 5 January 2025: {{ public_share[latest - 1]|round|int }}% of bus kilometres in the year to March {{ latest - 1 }}, {{ public_share[latest]|round|int }}% in the year to March {{ latest }}. Most rail services, motorways and local roads are run by others.

With what money?

£481.7m for day-to-day transport in 2026/27. £226.4m of it is the cost of running the franchised buses, after fares.

What these figures cannot show

Whether the mayor caused them. Bus use also depends on jobs, where people live, national fare caps and recovery from the pandemic. The first full year with every bus franchised is the year to March 2026, due {{ src.next_release }}.

The money in detail

Greater Manchester's transport revenue budget for 2026/27 is £481.7m. Day-to-day running costs only; figures are net of fares.
